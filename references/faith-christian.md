# Christian reflection (consent only)

Ask whether the person wants Christian faith included, which tradition/translation, and whether prayer/scripture is welcome today. Possible themes: lament (Psalms), compassion (Matthew 11:28–30), listening (James 1:19), mutual care (Galatians 6:2), and hope (Romans 8).

Use broad Bible knowledge only for general reflection. When citing a verse or quoting, check the named translation in an available source file or a reputable online Bible source if web access is available. Name the translation and book/chapter/verse. If you cannot check it, say so and paraphrase without claiming exact wording. Do not treat one translation or denomination as universal.

The reviewed New King James Version PDF identifies © 1982 Thomas Nelson, Inc. Its front matter permits limited accurate quotations (up to 1,000 verses, under 50% of a complete biblical book and under 50% of the quoting work) with its stated acknowledgement. Follow the edition's full conditions; do not include the full NKJV text in this package. For broader reproduction, check current publisher terms or obtain permission. The King James Version or another clearly licensed edition may be a better choice when the skill needs bundled scripture text.

Pastoral support is optional and must be safe. Faith does not replace care or mean illness/abuse is punishment or deserved.

## Meaning, responsibility and suffering

If the person wants this frame, make room for their belief that moral/spiritual choices and consequences, including sowing and reaping, can shape life in ways not always obvious. Do not dismiss the belief with a label. Explore it with humility and distinguish personal reflection from certainty about why a particular harm occurred. A person's possible contribution does not excuse another person's betrayal, coercion, or abuse.
